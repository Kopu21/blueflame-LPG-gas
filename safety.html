Emergency Action Protocol

What to Do If You Suspect an LPG Leak

Ethyl mercaptan gives LPG its distinct pungent warning odor. If you smell gas, remain calm and follow these 6 non-negotiable life-saving steps in exact order.

Emergency Helpline: 1800-233-3555
01 Strict Prohibition

No Matches or Open Flames

Do not light matchsticks, candles, incense sticks, or cigarettes. Never test for gas leaks using a lighter or flame under any circumstances.

02 Spark Prevention

Do Not Operate Electrical Switches

Do NOT touch light switches, exhaust fans, doorbells, or electrical appliances. Flipping switches generates micro-sparks that can ignite gas vapor.

03 Fuel Cutoff

Turn Off Regulator & Valve

If it is safe to approach, immediately turn the cylinder regulator switch clockwise to the OFF position and cap the valve to isolate the supply.

04 Floor Dispersion

Open Doors & Windows

Throw open all kitchen windows and outer doors to establish cross-ventilation. LPG is heavier than air and sinks; natural air current sweeps it outdoors.

05 Safe Evacuation

Keep People Away & Evacuate

Evacuate all household occupants, children, and pets immediately to open ground. Do not re-enter the dwelling until the odor has completely vanished.

06 Professional Dispatch

Call Distributor from Safe Distance

Step into open air outside the building and call our 24/7 Agency Helpline at 011-2440-9900 or National Emergency 1906 for instant technical response.

Standard Testing Method

The Soap Bubble Leak Inspection

Never use an open flame to detect gas leaks. The only authorized household and commercial testing method is applying rich soap-water foam around the cylinder valve nozzle, regulator locking joint, and rubber hose fittings.

No Bubbles (100% Airtight): The soapy foam remains static without foaming or expansion. The internal O-ring and brass connection collar are completely sealed and safe for cooking.
Growing Foam / Expanding Bubbles: Gas is escaping from the joint. Immediately turn off the regulator, unclamp it, check the internal O-ring seal, and refit firmly until bubbles vanish.

01 • Setup Protocol

Safe LPG Cylinder Installation Standards

Proper cylinder positioning is the fundamental pillar of household safety. A compliant installation ensures natural ventilation, eliminates thermal stress, and guarantees immediate access during emergencies.

  • Keep Cylinder Strictly Upright: Always place the cylinder vertically upright on a firm, flat, level floor or certified roller stand. Never tilt, slant, or invert a cylinder.
  • Maintain Ample Ground-Level Ventilation: Install in an airy space with unrestricted low-level air movement. Never box cylinders inside sealed, airtight cabinets without ventilation louvers.
  • Safe Distance from Heat & Sparks: Keep the cylinder at least 1 meter away from burning gas stoves, electric heaters, ovens, power outlets, and combustible chemicals.
  • Audible Locking Collar Engagement: When attaching the regulator, press down firmly until the quick-release collar locks with an audible click, ensuring an airtight seal.
  • Keep Installation Area Free & Clear: Maintain clear passage around the cylinder. Never stack cooking utensils, grocery bags, or cleaning supplies around the valve.

02 • Hardware Care

LPG Hose & Regulator Maintenance

Rubber hoses and pressure regulators endure continuous fuel friction and kitchen grease. Systematic inspection and adhering to certified replacement intervals prevents micro-fissures and joint degradation.

  • Monthly Visual Surface Inspection: Inspect the flexible orange Suraksha hose for micro-cracks, stiffness, chemical softening, heat hardening, or rodent bite abrasions.
  • Check Internal Valve O-Ring Seal: Before attaching a new refill, inspect the inner rubber O-ring gasket inside the cylinder valve neck. If dry, torn, or displaced, replace it immediately.
  • Replace Every 5 Years via Authorized Source: ISI-certified steel-wire braided Suraksha hoses have a mandatory 5-year service life. Never purchase unbranded local plastic tubes.
  • Avoid Bending, Twisting & Heavy Loads: Route the safety hose gently without sharp 90-degree corners, kinks, or heavy cutting boards and pots pressing against the rubber jacket.
  • Zero DIY Repairs: Never attempt temporary fixes with electrical tape, sealants, or improvised clamps. Gas pressures require certified factory-molded fittings.

03 • Storage Standards

Safe Cylinder Storage & Handling Precautions

Whether storing a backup DBC domestic refill or managing commercial cylinder banks, proper physical containment isolates fuel from thermal expansion risks and mechanical impact.

  • Dedicated Well-Ventilated Storage: Store spare cylinders in cool, dry, ground-level spaces with cross-ventilation. Protect cylinder shells from rain and direct sunlight.
  • Maintain Electrical Isolation: Keep storage zones completely clear of electrical switchgear, high-amp circuits, battery inverters, and combustible items.
  • Never in Basements or Sub-Pits: Because LPG is denser than ambient air, any minor leak accumulates in low pits and basements, posing extreme explosion risks.
  • Avoid Dropping, Dragging or Rolling: Always lift cylinders using the welded protective collar handles. Never roll cylinders horizontally along road surfaces.
  • Keep Safety Cap Firmly Attached: When a cylinder is disconnected or held in reserve, keep the molded plastic safety cap securely tethered over the brass valve.

04 • Daily Best Practices

Everyday LPG Safety Habits for Peace of Mind

Safety is a continuous routine. Establishing five simple daily discipline steps transforms domestic cooking into an accident-free family experience.

01

Turn Off Burner Knobs

Always rotate stove burner knobs clockwise to OFF immediately once meal preparation concludes.

02

Close Night Regulator

Switch off the cylinder regulator knob every night before sleeping or whenever vacating the home.

03

Keep Flammables Away

Keep cooking oils, kitchen towels, curtains, paper boxes, and plastic bottles at least 3 feet from burners.

04

Never Leave Unattended

Never leave simmering pans or boiling milk unattended; boiled-over liquids can blow out the flame while gas flows.

Child-Safe Kitchen Rule: Keep children away from cylinder valves and stove knobs; install flame-retardant safety knob guards if needed.

Handling Essentials

Household LPG Safety Do's and Don'ts

Adhere strictly to certified safety guidelines approved by the Petroleum and Explosives Safety Organisation.

Recommended Safety Do's

  • Always keep the LPG cylinder in an upright, vertical position on a flat, level surface.
  • Ensure adequate natural ventilation around the kitchen floor and cylinder storage area.
  • Turn off the cylinder regulator switch whenever the stove is not in use, especially at night.
  • Use certified steel-wire reinforced orange Suraksha rubber hoses and replace every 5 years.
  • Verify delivery tare weight and security seal intactness before accepting cylinder delivery.

Strict Safety Don'ts

  • Never roll, slide, or store an LPG cylinder horizontally on its side or upside down.
  • Never use unapproved local plastic hoses or damaged rubber pipes showing cracks or hardening.
  • Never store kerosene, spirits, paint thinners, or flammable solvents in the cylinder room.
  • Never inspect for gas leaks using matchsticks, lighters, or open flames under any condition.
  • Never install cylinders in underground basements, unventilated cavities, or closed trenches.

Consumer Knowledge Desk

Frequently Asked LPG Safety Questions

Quick, reliable answers to essential household gas handling and hardware maintenance inquiries.

You should perform a visual inspection of your LPG rubber hose at least once every month to check for surface cracking, brittleness, or rodent nibbles. Under BIS IS:9573 standards, an authentic steel-wire reinforced orange Suraksha hose must be mandatorily replaced every 5 years, even if no visible wear is evident. Regulators should be inspected every 5 to 7 years or replaced immediately if the quick-release locking collar becomes wobbly or stiff.
Do NOT touch any electrical light switches, exhaust fans, doorbells, or telephone handsets. Do not light matchsticks or lighters. Immediately turn the cylinder regulator switch clockwise to the OFF position, open all doors and windows wide to let gas disperse outdoors, evacuate all family members and pets into fresh air, and call our 24/7 emergency dispatch helpline at 1800-233-3555 from an outdoor location.
LPG is stored inside the cylinder under pressure as a liquid with vapor occupying the upper headspace. When kept upright, only vapor enters the low-pressure regulator. If placed horizontally on its side, liquid gas enters the regulator, which can rupture the internal diaphragm, flood the rubber hose, and cause a sudden, uncontrollable high-pressure liquid flare-up.
Common indicators of a damaged regulator include: a subtle hissing sound near the brass connection joint, a loose fit that wobbles when touched, a jammed or excessively stiff on/off rotating switch, gas odor lingering around the valve collar, or lazy, pulsating yellow flames on the stove. If any of these signs appear, stop using the gas connection immediately and request an authorized technician checkup.
Never attempt DIY repairs on gas hoses or fittings. Adhesives, electrical insulation tapes, and industrial glues dissolve rapidly when exposed to hydrocarbon LPG vapors, and internal operating pressure will quickly blow through the tape, leading to a catastrophic gas leak. Always contact your authorized distributor for a genuine ISI-stamped Suraksha hose replacement.
